Jyutping Guide

How to read Cantonese romanization

What Is Jyutping?

Jyutping is the standard romanization system for Cantonese. It uses the letters a-z to represent sounds, plus a number from 1 to 6 to indicate the tone. Every Cantonese syllable in this course is written in Jyutping.

nei5
n = consonant (initial) ei = vowel (final) 5 = tone number

This syllable means "you" (你). The consonant tells you how to start the sound, the vowel gives the body of the sound, and the tone number tells you the pitch pattern.

A Jyutping syllable has up to three parts: an initial (consonant at the start), a final (vowel sound, sometimes ending in a consonant), and a tone number. Some syllables have no initial — they start directly with the vowel.

Initials (Consonant Sounds)

These are the consonants that can start a syllable. Most sound close to their English equivalents, with a few surprises.

Jyutping Sounds like Example
b Like English "b" baa4 baa1 (dad)
p Like English "p" with a puff of air (aspirated) po4 (wife, in lou5 po4)
m Like English "m" maa4 maa1 (mom)
f Like English "f" faan6 (rice)
d Like English "d" dai6 (younger, in dai6 dai2)
t Like English "t" with a puff of air tai2 (to look)
n Like English "n" nei5 (you)
l Like English "l" lou5 (old, in lou5 gung1)
g Like English "g" gau2 (nine)
k Like English "k" with a puff of air keoi5 (he/she)
ng Like the "ng" in "sing" — but at the start ng5 (five), ngo5 (I)
h Like English "h" hou2 (good)
gw Like "g" + "w" together gwai3 (expensive)
kw Like "k" + "w" together kwong3 (bright)
w Like English "w" wui5 (will/can)
j Like English "y" (not "j"!) jat1 (one), ji6 (two)
s Like English "s" saam1 (three), sei3 (four)
c Like "ts" with a puff of air (as in "cats") cat1 (seven)
z Like "dz" (as in "adds") zou2 (early, in zou2 san4)

The biggest trap: Jyutping j sounds like English "y", and Jyutping c sounds like "ts". Don't read them as English j and c.

Tone Numbers

The number at the end of each Jyutping syllable tells you the pitch pattern. There are six tones in Cantonese. For the full details, see the Tone Chart.

#NamePatternExample
1High LevelHigh, flatsaam1 (three)
2High RisingMid to highgau2 (nine)
3Mid LevelMid, flatsei3 (four)
4Low FallingMid to lowsi4 (time)
5Low RisingLow to midnei5 (you)
6Low LevelLow, flatji6 (two)

You do not need to memorize all initials and finals right away. Learn them as you encounter new words. The important thing is the tone number — always pay attention to it. A wrong consonant might still be understood; a wrong tone becomes a different word entirely.
