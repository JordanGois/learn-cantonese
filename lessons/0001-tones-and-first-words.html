Lesson 1

Tones Are Everything

The single most important thing about Cantonese

In English, pitch shows emotion. A rising voice means you are asking a question. A falling voice means you are making a statement. The words stay the same.

Cantonese is completely different. In Cantonese, pitch changes the word itself. The syllable "si" said six different ways produces six different words with six unrelated meanings. Get the tone wrong and you are literally saying a different word.

The Six Tones

Every Cantonese syllable has a tone, marked by a number 1-6 after the romanisation (called Jyutping). Here they all are:

Don't try to memorise all six right now. The goal for today is to understand that tones exist and to start hearing the difference between high, mid, and low. We will drill each tone pair in the lessons ahead.

Your First Words

Four phrases you can use today. Each word card shows the Jyutping romanisation, the Chinese characters, and the meaning. Press the button to hear the pitch pattern.

你好
nei5 hou2
Hello
nei5 starts low and rises (T5). hou2 rises higher (T2). Think of the bounce in an enthusiastic "Hi there!"
多謝
do1 ze6
Thank you (for a gift or favour)
do1 is high and flat (T1). ze6 drops to a low flat note (T6). High then low — like setting something down gently.
唔該
m4 goi1
Thank you (for service) / Excuse me
m4 drops low (T4), then goi1 jumps back up high (T1). Use this when someone holds the door or brings your food.
我愛你
ngo5 oi3 nei5
I love you
ngo5 rises from low (T5), oi3 sits in the middle (T3), nei5 rises from low again (T5). A gentle wave pattern.

Quick Check

Three quick questions to make sure the core idea landed.

Practice With Your Partner

Try this tonight

  1. Say nei5 hou2 to your partner. Ask them if your tones sound right.
  2. Use m4 goi1 next time they hand you something or do you a favour.
  3. Say ngo5 oi3 nei5 before bed. Even if it sounds wobbly, they will appreciate it.

Don't worry about being perfect. The goal is to start using the sounds. Your partner is the best teacher you have.
