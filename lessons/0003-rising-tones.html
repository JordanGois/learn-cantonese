Lesson 3

Rising Tones

The tones that go up — and why they're different

In Lessons 1 and 2, you learned the three level tones: T1 (high), T3 (mid), and T6 (low). They sit on shelves — high, mid, low — and stay flat.

Today we add the two rising tones: T2 (high rising) and T5 (low rising). Same direction — up — but different starting points.

Two Types of Rise

Tone 2 (high rising) starts at mid pitch and rises to high. Think of someone surprised, asking "Really?" in English. The voice lifts from the middle to the top.

Tone 5 (low rising) starts at low pitch and rises to mid. Like a questioning "hmm?" — lower energy than T2. The voice lifts from the bottom, but only reaches the middle.

The key difference: same direction (up), but different starting points. T2 starts higher and ends higher than T5.

Listen for where the tone starts. T2 begins in the middle and soars up; T5 begins low and only reaches the middle.

Practice With Your Partner

  • Say nei5 hou2 focusing on the two rises. Feel nei5 rise from low-to-mid, then hou2 rise from mid-to-high.
  • Ask your partner to say gau2 (nine) and jyu5 (rain) — listen for the different starting points of each rise.
  • Practice mou5 in context: say "ngo5 mou5" (I don't have). Both syllables are T5 — two low rises in a row.
