Lesson 10

Yes/No Questions

How to ask — and the Cantonese way to say yes

You have already seen one question pattern: nei5 hou2 maa3? (Lesson 6). Now learn the full picture of yes/no questions — including a surprising fact: Cantonese has no word for "yes."

Pattern 1: The maa3 Particle

The simplest way to ask a yes/no question: take any statement and add maa3 at the end.

你好嗎？
nei5 hou2 maa3?
Are you good? / How are you?
You already know this one from Lesson 6!
佢係你嘅哥哥嗎？
keoi5 hai6 nei5 ge3 go1 go1 maa3?
Is he your older brother?
Statement + maa3 = yes/no question. That is it.
你食咗嗎？
nei5 sik6 zo2 maa3?
Have you eaten?
A classic Cantonese greeting — used like "How are you?" in everyday life!

Pattern 2: A-not-A Questions

Take the verb, repeat it with m4 (not) in the middle. This creates a "verb or not verb?" pattern that is more direct than maa3.

係唔係？
hai6 m4 hai6?
Is or isn't? / Right?
The most common A-not-A question. Used constantly.
好唔好？
hou2 m4 hou2?
Good or not good? / Okay?
Used when asking if something is acceptable or if someone agrees.

Example with a full sentence:

佢係唔係你嘅媽媽？
keoi5 hai6 m4 hai6 nei5 ge3 maa4 maa1?
Is she or isn't she your mom?

Both patterns ask yes/no questions. maa3 is softer; A-not-A is more direct, used when you want a clear answer.

The "No Yes" Surprise

Cantonese does not have a single word for "yes"! To answer "yes," you repeat the verb from the question:

  • hai6 m4 hai6? → Answer "yes": hai6 (it is)
  • hou2 m4 hou2? → Answer "yes": hou2 (it's good)
  • nei5 sik6 zo2 maa3? → Answer "yes": sik6 zo2 (ate)

To say "no," add m4 before the verb:

  • m4 hai6 — it's not
  • m4 hou2 — not good
  • mei6 — not yet (for "have you...?" questions)

This feels strange at first but becomes natural quickly. Think of it this way: instead of a generic "yes," you give a specific confirmation by echoing the verb.

Practice With Your Partner

  • Ask your partner 5 yes/no questions using both patterns (maa3 and A-not-A).
  • Practice the classic greeting: "nei5 sik6 zo2 faan6 mei6?" (Have you eaten rice yet?) — one of the most common Cantonese greetings!
  • Listen to how your partner answers — notice they repeat the verb, not say a separate "yes" word.
  • Try asking about family: "keoi5 hai6 m4 hai6 nei5 ge3 go1 go1?" (Is he your older brother?)
