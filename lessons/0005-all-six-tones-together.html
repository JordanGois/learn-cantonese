Lesson 5

All Six Tones Together

The full picture — hear, sort, and recall all six

You now know all six Cantonese tones. Three are level — the pitch stays flat at different heights. Two are rising — the pitch climbs. One is falling — the pitch drops.

  • T1 — High level
  • T2 — High rising
  • T3 — Mid level
  • T4 — Low falling
  • T5 — Low rising
  • T6 — Low level

This lesson introduces nothing new. Instead, you will integrate everything from Phase 1 through heavy retrieval practice: hearing, sorting, flipping, and quizzing.

Memory Framework

"Three shelves, three movements." This is the mnemonic that holds the whole system together.

The Shelves (level tones)

  • High shelf (T1) — steady pitch at the top of your range
  • Mid shelf (T3) — steady pitch at your normal speaking level
  • Low shelf (T6) — steady pitch at the bottom of your range

The Movements (contour tones)

  • High Rise (T2) — starts at mid, climbs to high
  • Low Rise (T5) — starts at low, climbs to mid
  • Low Fall (T4) — starts at mid, drops to low

Three shelves where pitch sits still. Three movements where pitch goes somewhere. That is the entire Cantonese tone system.

Practice With Your Partner

Tone graduation

  1. Say the full si1, si2, si3, si4, si5, si6 set out loud. Use the "Play all 6" button above as a reference.
  2. Ask your partner to say a random "si" word and see if you can identify the tone number.
  3. Go back to all four phrases from Lesson 1 — nei5 hou2, do1 ze6, m4 goi1, ngo5 oi3 nei5 — and say them with correct tones.
  4. Ask your partner to rate your tones on a scale of 1–5. Anything above a 3 means you are ready for Phase 2.
