Grammar Notes

Patterns you've learned so far

Basic Sentence Order

Subject + Verb + Object

Subject + Verb + Object

Cantonese basic word order is the same as English: the subject comes first, then the verb, then the object.

我愛你
ngo5 oi3 nei5
I love you
我食飯
ngo5 sik6 faan6
I eat rice

hai6 (係) — To Be

Subject + hai6 + Noun

Subject + hai6 + Noun

Use hai6 to equate the subject with a noun. It works like English "is/am/are".

佢係我嘅媽媽
keoi5 hai6 ngo5 ge3 maa4 maa1
She is my mom
Note: hai6 links a subject to a noun, not an adjective. You would not normally say "ngo5 hai6 hou2" for "I am good." Adjectives work differently in Cantonese — more on this in later lessons.

ge3 (嘅) — Possessive

Pronoun + ge3 = possessive

Pronoun + ge3

Add ge3 after a pronoun to make it possessive, like adding "'s" in English.

ngo5 ge3
my / mine
nei5 ge3
your / yours
keoi5 ge3
his / her / its

dei6 (哋) — Plural Marker

Pronoun + dei6 = plural

Pronoun + dei6

Add dei6 after a singular pronoun to make it plural.

ngo5 dei6
we / us
nei5 dei6
you (plural)
keoi5 dei6
they / them

Questions with maa3 (嗎)

Statement + maa3 = yes/no question

Statement + maa3?

The simplest way to ask a yes/no question: take any statement and add maa3 at the end.

你好嗎？
nei5 hou2 maa3?
Are you good? / How are you?
Comparison: This is similar to adding a question mark in English, except maa3 is spoken aloud. Think of it as the spoken version of "?".

A-not-A Questions

Verb + m4 + Verb

Verb + m4 + Verb?

Another way to ask yes/no questions: repeat the verb with m4 (not) in between. This literally asks "is it or isn't it?"

係唔係？
hai6 m4 hai6?
Is it or isn't it?
好唔好？
hou2 m4 hou2?
Good or not good? / Is it okay?

Answering Yes / No

Repeat the verb (yes) or m4 + verb (no)

Yes: repeat the verb   |   No: m4 + verb

Cantonese has no single word for "yes" or "no." Instead, you repeat the verb from the question to say yes, or put m4 before it to say no.

hai6
Yes (it is) — literally "is"
m4 hai6
No (it isn't) — literally "not is"
hou2
Yes (it's good)
m4 hou2
No (it's not good)

m4 (唔) — Negation

m4 + Verb = not

m4 + Verb

Place m4 directly before any verb to negate it. This is the most common negation in Cantonese.

m4 hai6
is not
m4 hou2
not good
m4 sik6
not eat / don't eat
m4 oi3
not love / don't love
Pattern summary: Cantonese grammar is simpler than you might expect. No verb conjugation, no articles (a/the), no plural endings on nouns. The main things to learn are word order and particles like ge3, maa3, and m4.
