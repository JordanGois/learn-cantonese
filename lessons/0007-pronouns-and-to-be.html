Lesson 7

Pronouns & "To Be"

Building your first real sentences

You can greet people and ask how they are. Now it is time to learn to talk about people. Good news: Cantonese pronouns are simpler than English — there is no gender for he/she!

The Pronouns

Singular Pronouns

我
ngo5
I / me
T5 (low rising). You already know this from Lesson 1.
你
nei5
you
T5 (low rising). Also from Lesson 1 — nei5 hou2!
佢
keoi5
he / she / it
T5 (low rising) again! No gender distinction. The same word covers he, she, and it.

All three basic pronouns are T5 (low rising). That makes them easy to remember — one tone for all three.

Plural Pronouns

Add dei6 (T6) after any singular pronoun to make it plural. Simple as that.

我哋
ngo5 dei6
we / us
你哋
nei5 dei6
you all
佢哋
keoi5 dei6
they / them

The Verb hai6 (係) — "to be"

hai6 (T6, low level) means is / am / are. It works the same for all pronouns — no conjugation needed. English has "I am, you are, he is" but Cantonese just uses hai6 for everything.

係
hai6
to be / is / am / are

Pattern: Subject + hai6 + Noun

我係人
ngo5 hai6 jan4
I am a person
佢係我嘅朋友
keoi5 hai6 ngo5 ge3 pang4 jau5
He/she is my friend

The Possessive ge3 (嘅)

ge3 (T3, mid level) makes things possessive, like the English 's. Place it right after the pronoun.

我嘅
ngo5 ge3
my / mine
你嘅
nei5 ge3
your / yours
佢嘅
keoi5 ge3
his / her / its

New word: pang4 jau5 (朋友) means "friend." You will see it in the exercises below.

Practice With Your Partner

  • Point at people in photos and say keoi5 hai6... sentences. "He/she is a ___."
  • Practice ngo5 ge3 (my) while pointing at your things. "This is my phone. This is my book."
  • Tell your partner: nei5 hai6 ngo5 ge3 pang4 jau5 — "You are my friend." (Or something nicer!)
