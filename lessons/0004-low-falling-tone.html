Lesson 4

The Low Falling Tone

The sighing tone — and how to tell it from low level

So far you know five tones: T1, T3, T6 (level) and T2, T5 (rising). One more to go: T4 (low falling). After this lesson, you'll know all six Cantonese tones!

The Sighing Tone

Tone 4 starts at mid-low pitch and falls to low. Think of sighing "oh..." in disappointment. The voice drops, like letting out a breath.

The tricky part: T4 and T6 are both in the low register. T4 falls, T6 stays flat. This is the hardest distinction for beginners — but your ear will learn it with practice.

Hear T4

T4 — Low Falling
T6 — Low Level
T1 — High Level

T4 starts slightly higher and slides down. T6 stays flat at the bottom. Play them back-to-back until you can hear the fall.

si4 falls; si6 stays flat. Both are low — can you hear the difference?

Practice With Your Partner

  • Ask your partner to alternate si4 (time) and si6 (matter). Can you hear the fall in si4 vs. the flat si6?
  • Practice m4 goi1 with correct T4 on m4 — let the "m" sigh downward before the crisp high T1 on goi1.
  • Try saying all the T4 words in a row: si4, m4, cin4, naam4. Feel the sigh in each one.
